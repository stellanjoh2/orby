Export

PNG

Raster snapshot of the current view—lighting, materials, and post effects included. Toggle transparency to drop the background; size is relative to the viewer—1× matches your current screen resolution, 2× exports at double that (larger file, more detail).

SVG

Vector output derived from the current framing—great for logos, precision edges, and scaling without pixels. Silhouette traces a single outline; Color builds flat filled regions from the render.

.GLB

Exports the extruded mesh as a compact glTF binary for use in other 3D tools, game engines, and AR viewers. Available when the loaded asset is an imported SVG extrusion.

Feature FAQ

Transform Widgets

Move, rotate, and scale your 3D model with visual gizmos. Click the mesh to activate rotate widgets, or use W/E/Q keys to toggle move, rotate, and scale controls. Perfect for positioning your model exactly where you want it.

Display Modes

Shaded: Shows your model with realistic lighting and materials. Unlit: Displays your model's textures and colors without any lighting effects—everything appears evenly lit, like a flat illustration. Clay: Renders your model in a single color, great for focusing on shape and form. Wireframe: Shows only the edges and structure of your model. You can also enable a wireframe overlay on any display mode, or hide the mesh entirely to see only the wireframe.

Material Controls

Brightness: Makes your model lighter or darker. Metalness: Controls how metallic the surface looks (0 = plastic, 1 = chrome). Roughness: Adjusts surface smoothness (0 = mirror-like, 1 = matte). Emissive: Adds glowing light to materials, perfect for sci-fi effects, neon signs, or any glowing elements.

Fresnel

Adds a colored glow around the edges of your model when viewed at an angle. Think of it like rim lighting that follows the silhouette. Great for making objects pop or creating a stylized look.

HDRI (Environment)

A 360° image that wraps around your scene, providing realistic lighting and reflections. Choose from different environments (meadow, studio, sunset) to change the mood and lighting of your render. The HDRI acts as both a light source and a background.

3-Point Lighting

Professional lighting setup with four lights: Key (main light), Fill (softens shadows), Rim (separates object from background), and Ambient (overall scene brightness). Adjust each light's color, intensity, and position to create the perfect look.

Post-Processing Effects

Bloom: Adds a glowing, dreamy effect to bright areas. Depth of Field: Blurs the background while keeping your model sharp (like a camera lens). Film Grain: Adds texture for a cinematic feel. Chromatic Aberration: Splits color channels slightly at the edges. Lens Flare: Realistic light flares from bright sources. Lens Dirt: Simulates smudges on a camera lens for atmosphere. Color & Tone (contrast, temperature, H/S/L, highlights/shadows, clarity, fade, sharpness, vignette) and tone mapping (e.g. ACES) sit in the Camera tab alongside exposure.

Camera Controls

Orbit: Click and drag to rotate around your model. Pan: Right-click and drag to move the view. Zoom: Scroll to zoom in/out. Focus: Press F to automatically frame your model. Auto-Orbit: Automatically orbits the camera around your model with smooth multi-axis movement—perfect for screensaver-style presentations. Press Esc to exit. Exposure & Auto Exposure: Manual exposure sets overall brightness; optional auto exposure adapts to the scene. Use the Histogram and Curve fold-outs, Look filters, and the rest of Color & Tone for a full grade.

Histogram

A graph showing the brightness distribution in your image. Helps you see if your render is too dark, too bright, or overexposed. The histogram turns orange or red when parts of your image are too bright (overexposed), warning you to lower the exposure. Toggle it on from the Camera tab; it sits in a fold-out under the heading, same idea as the curve (see below).

Tone curve

A luminance response curve with two draggable control points (smooth PCHIP interpolation). It shapes how shadows and highlights map before the rest of color grading—use it for contrast roll-off, lifted blacks, or punchy mids. Open the Curve fold-out under Camera (next to the histogram); your curve is saved in Copy scene / .orby like other settings.

Look filter presets

Under Camera → Filters, pick a thumbnail preset (Studio, Noir, Golden, etc.) for a one-tap look: color grade, tone curve, bloom, grain, chromatic aberration, and more are applied together. None resets those camera/FX defaults while keeping framing (FOV, tilt, orbit). If you tweak sliders afterward, the selection shows as custom. Preset state is included when you copy or paste scene JSON.

Podium & Grid

Add a platform (podium) or grid floor to your scene. Great for product presentations or architectural renders. The podium can be scaled and colored, and the grid helps with alignment and perspective.

Scene Settings

Copy and paste all your settings (lighting, camera, effects, materials, tone curve, look filter selection, histogram/curve panel toggles, and more) as JSON. Perfect for saving your favorite setups or sharing them with others. Load a scene to instantly apply all settings at once.

SVG Extrude

Orby can convert SVG fills into 3D meshes. After importing an SVG, use the Object → SVG Extrude block to control form and shading.

  • Depth: Sets global extrusion depth for all SVG fills.
  • Angle: Controls normal smoothing (lower = faceted edges, higher = smoother highlights).
  • Flip Direction: Inverts front/back extrusion direction.
  • Color Override: Replaces imported SVG fill colors with a single chosen color.
  • Per-Color Depth/Position: Fine-tune each detected fill color independently for layered logo effects.
  • Scene Settings: SVG extrude controls are included when copying or pasting scene settings.
  • SVG to GLB Export: Exported SVG meshes are saved as opaque GLB materials for predictable shading across viewers.
Performance

Orby is a real-time GPU renderer built on WebGL and Three.js. Every frame, the scene is drawn and then post-processing effects are applied as full-screen “passes”. The more heavy effects you stack, and the higher your resolution, the more work your GPU has to do each frame.

  • Very cheap (almost free): exposure, contrast, saturation, tint, temperature, fade, and the luminance tone curve (evaluated in the same color pass). These are light math per pixel.
  • Medium cost: vignette, clarity, sharpness, Fresnel. These add extra calculations or an extra pass, but are usually fine on modern GPUs.
  • Heavier effects: depth of field, bloom, lens dirt, film grain, chromatic aberration, FXAA, and some histogram/auto-exposure work. These often require additional buffers and multiple full-screen passes and will have the biggest impact on frame rate.

Turning an effect on is usually the biggest step in cost. Increasing intensity (for example, bloom strength from 0.3 to 0.6) changes the look more than the performance. The real performance lever is how many heavy effects you run at the same time.

If Orby feels slow, start by disabling the heavier effects (Depth of Field, Bloom, Lens Dirt, Grain, Chromatic Aberration, FXAA), or run the browser window a bit smaller so the internal render resolution is lower. For quick look-dev, stick to the cheaper color & tone controls first, then enable the cinematic effects at the end when you are close to final.

About Orby

Orby is a completely free, zero-install, drag-and-drop 3D model viewer that makes any model look stunning in seconds. It's perfect for AI-generated 3D (Meshy, Tripo, Luma, CSM, etc.), game assets, product visualization, and everything in between.

No accounts, no waiting, no uploads—just drop your file and get a full artist-grade studio with HDR environments, look filter presets, a luminance tone curve, real depth of field, selective bloom, film grain, tonemapping, 3-point lighting, and pro controls that react instantly.

This project is an ongoing experiment in vibe-coding and self-directed learning. Coming from a design background with no formal development experience, I use Orby as a canvas to explore modern web technologies and real-time 3D on the web.
